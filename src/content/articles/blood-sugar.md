# “My sugar” is usually three different tests

People say “my sugar was high” and mean a fingerstick, a lab slip, or a number someone mentioned at a health fair. Those are not the same measurement.

### What is A1C?

A1C estimates average blood sugar over about three months. It does not tell you what happened after lunch today. Some conditions change how reliable it is, which is one reason a clinician may want a different test as well. The [CDC’s overview of diabetes testing](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/diabetes-testing/index.html) is a plain place to see how the common tests differ.

### What about a fasting number?

A fasting plasma glucose is a lab test after you have not eaten. A random glucose is a lab test without that wait. If you were told to fast and you had coffee with milk, say so. The number is still a number. It is just a different one.

### And the meter at home?

A home meter helps people who already have diabetes, or who were asked to check. It is not automatically the test used to decide whether someone has diabetes. Bring the log if you have one. Dates matter more than the single highest reading you remember.

Thirst, urinating much more than usual, blurry vision, or weight loss you cannot explain are reasons to call a clinician rather than wait for a routine checkup. Trouble breathing, vomiting, or confusion with those symptoms is a reason to seek emergency care the same day.

If you are worried because a parent has diabetes and you have never been checked, that is enough to say when you request a visit. You do not need the word prediabetes to start.
