# One number for a household that has too many

On a weekday morning in Reseda the people in a community clinic are not all from the same block. Someone walked. Someone came off the 101 from Canoga Park. Someone is there for a parent who stayed in the car because the stairs were unclear. The useful thing a neighborhood clinic can be, when it works, is a single place that already knows those relationships.

Canby Community Clinic is that kind of office for this part of the Valley. Suite 6B, 7601 Canby Ave. It was Pura Vida Community Clinic before the name changed. The hours are weekdays, 9 AM to 5 PM. The phone is (818) 674-4414. English and Spanish.

## What “one place” can and cannot hold

A household does not have one body. A father’s pressure, a daughter’s checkup, a grandmother’s medicine list: those are separate visits with separate charts. What a single clinic can hold is the practical layer. The same front desk. The same phone number. A notebook you keep at home so you are not the only person who remembers which pharmacy was used last time.

It will not be the place that does every procedure. Mammograms, a lot of blood draws, and specialty care often happen at another office. The job here is to know that, say the name of the next place, and still be the number you call when the result comes back and nobody has explained it.

If you live far outside the west Valley, this may be the wrong drive. [HRSA’s Find a Health Center](https://findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov/) is a public locator for other clinics. Use it before you cross the city. Call this office if you are in Reseda, Winnetka, Northridge, Canoga Park, Lake Balboa, or Tarzana and you want to know whether the visit you need is one we do.

## Language is part of the care, not a courtesy

A missed word about a dose is not a small miss. If the person being seen thinks in Spanish, say that when you schedule, not after the plan has been explained once in English. If a child has been the interpreter, you can ask for a different arrangement. Children should not be carrying results, allergies, or a new medicine home in their head.

Write the five words you refuse to have summarized. The drug name. The reaction you had. The symptom you have been translating as “tired” because the real word feels like too much. Bring that list.

## A household notebook

One notebook for the household works if you keep the pages separate. Name at the top. Date. The reason for the visit. The next step, in one sentence. The phone number that was given for the lab or the other office. Do not let one person’s result become the family’s diagnosis. Blood pressure that runs high in a parent is a reason for the parent to be seen. It is not a diagnosis for everyone at the table.

Insurance cards get mixed up in the same bag. When you call, have the right card in your hand. If there is no card, say that. You can still ask for a visit.

## After urgent care, come back here

Families use urgent care because it is open when this office is not. That is the correct door for a Saturday sprain or a fever that will not wait until Monday. Bring the paper they gave you to the next weekday visit. Otherwise the sprain is treated and the blood pressure nobody mentioned is still unmeasured, and the chart at the clinic you actually want to use stays blank.

Walk in on a weekday, or leave a name and a phone number if you want a time held. The clinic calls you back. If you are calling for someone else, say whose visit it is and which language they want.

## Getting there, and getting the time right

Parking and the entrance are the questions people are embarrassed to ask, and then they are late. Call and ask which door, and whether there is a step. If someone in the family uses a wheelchair, say so before the day of the visit, not from the sidewalk.

Weekday hours are 9 to 5. If your shift ends at 5, you will not make a 4:40 visit that still needs check-in. Say the constraint when you request a time. A morning that actually works is more useful than a polite yes to an hour you will miss.

Children sometimes come along because there is nowhere else for them to be. Say that too. A visit can still happen. It goes better when the desk knows the stroller is part of the plan.

## When the household needs more than a clinic

Food, a shelter bed, a ride, help with a benefits application: those are not solved in an exam room. 211 LA is the public directory for that layer of the week. The clinic visit can sit beside it. A blood pressure check does not fix a housing problem, and a housing problem does not mean you skip the blood pressure. Families around here are often doing both errands in the same month. Keep the phone numbers in the same notebook and do not expect one front desk to be the other one.

If a neighbor asks you where to start, give them the suite number, not just “the clinic on Canby.” 6B is the detail that keeps people out of the wrong door.
