# You can call without a card in your wallet

Not having insurance is a reason people put off a visit. It is not a reason the phone will not be answered. At Canby Community Clinic in Reseda, say you do not have a card and say what kind of visit you need. A checkup, blood pressure, a medicine question, follow-up of something you already know about.

## What helps the person on the phone

Your name, a number that you answer, the language you want the visit in, and whether you are new. If you have had Medi-Cal before and you are not sure it is still active, say that too. Do not read a member number into a website form. The form is for a callback, not for records.

Bring whatever you do have. A list of medicines, or the bottles. Old lab papers if they are in a drawer. A photo ID if you have one. None of that is required before anyone will talk to you. It just makes the visit shorter.

## Coverage is a different errand

Applying for Medi-Cal or a marketplace plan does not happen at the front desk of this website. [Covered California](https://www.coveredca.com/) is the state’s marketplace, and it includes information about Medi-Cal. [BenefitsCal](https://benefitscal.com/) is where many people start an application. 211 LA can help if the forms themselves are the problem.

You can do those applications and still be seen. Tell the clinic which one you are in the middle of, if you know. The visit and the paperwork are related. They are not the same appointment.

## What to say, in order

“I don’t have insurance. I need a checkup” is a complete opening. So is “I don’t have insurance. My pressure at home has been high and I want someone to look at it.” You do not have to narrate a medical history to the website, and you should not. Symptoms, old diagnoses, and photos of paperwork belong on the phone or in the room.

If a friend told you the visit works a certain way, ask the clinic rather than arriving with that version. Hours are weekdays, 9 AM to 5 PM. The phone is (818) 674-4414. Walk in during those hours, or leave your name and a number if you want someone to call you back and hold a time.

211 is the public line if what you actually need today is food, a ride, or help with an application, and a medical visit is the second errand. The clinic can still be the first call. It should not be the only number in your phone.
