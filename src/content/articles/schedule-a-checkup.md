# A checkup is worth it when something has gone stale

People request a checkup for a reason, even when the reason is only “it has been a while.” These are the ones that usually change what happens in the room.

- You cannot remember the last general visit. Blood pressure, medicines, and which screening fits your age all go out of date quietly.
- Home readings keep landing high. A cuff on the kitchen table is useful. It is more useful if someone looks at the pattern with you.
- A medicine was started, stopped, or doubled, and nobody has checked how you are doing on it.
- Someone in the family was diagnosed with diabetes, heart disease, or cancer, and you are not sure whether that changes your own plan.
- A symptom has been around long enough that you have started explaining it away. Fatigue, sleep, a headache pattern, a change in weight, bowel or bladder habits.

A checkup does not have to include every test on a poster. A focused visit can be blood pressure, the medicines you take, the two questions you wrote down, and a decision about what, if anything, comes next.

If you want a time set aside, call the clinic or leave your name and phone. Someone calls you back. You can also come on a weekday during open hours.
