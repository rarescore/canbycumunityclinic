# Three different doors

People lose time because the words all sound like “the doctor.” Emergency care, urgent care, and a weekday clinic are built for different problems. Using the wrong one is not a character flaw. The signs are just easy to talk yourself out of.

## Call 911

Chest pain or pressure. Trouble breathing. Face drooping, arm weakness, trouble speaking. Severe bleeding. Fainting. Confusion that is new. A serious injury. A headache that is sudden and the worst you have had, especially with other neurologic changes. Do not drive yourself to a routine appointment to see if it passes. Do not send a website form.

If you are unsure and the symptom is one of those, treat it as an emergency. A dispatcher is more useful than an article.

## Urgent care

Urgent care is for problems that should not wait until next week and are not the list above. A sprain. A fever that worries you tonight. A cut that may need stitches. An ear pain that blew up on a Saturday. Those clinics are built for the same day. They are not built to be your record over the next year.

## A weekday clinic

A checkup. Follow-up of a condition that is already known. Blood pressure. A question about a medicine. Help arranging a test a clinician orders. Something that has been bothering you for weeks and is not on the 911 list.

Canby Community Clinic does that kind of visit at 7601 Canby Ave #6B in Reseda, Monday through Friday, 9 AM to 5 PM. The phone is (818) 674-4414. You can walk in during those hours, or leave your name and phone if you want a time held. The clinic calls you back. It is closed at night and on weekends, so a problem that cannot wait until morning is one of the other two doors.

After an emergency visit or urgent care, the story still needs a home. Bring the discharge paper to a weekday visit. The value is often the follow-up, not the first stop.
