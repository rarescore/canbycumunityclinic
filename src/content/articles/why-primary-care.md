# The clinic that still has last year’s note

A lot of care happens in pieces. A blood pressure at a pharmacy. A lab from a job screening. A prescription from a visit you barely remember. None of those pieces is useless. They just do not talk to each other.

A regular clinic is the place that keeps the sequence. The person who sees you can look at the number from last spring, the medicine that was started after it, and the question you meant to ask and forgot. That is the whole argument. Not a longer visit. A visit that starts further along.

People notice it when a medicine changes. The bottle in the cabinet and the bottle you actually take are often different. A clinic that has seen you before will ask which one is true. A new office, meeting you once, usually takes the list you brought and moves on.

The same thing happens with screening. The useful question is not “what tests exist.” It is “which one is due for you, and what happened the last time.” Age, family history, and an old result change the answer. Someone has to be holding those facts between visits.

If you have been using whichever office could see you that week, you are not doing it wrong. Work and rides and school hours decide a lot of it. When you can, pick one place and take the papers with you. The second visit is usually shorter than the first, because the story is already there.
