# The useful bag is smaller than people think

A first visit gets described as a thick packet. Most of the time the bag by the door only needs a few things, and one of them is usually still in the bathroom cabinet.

Put the medicines in. Prescription bottles, the ones you buy without a prescription, vitamins, and anything a relative told you to take. If you cannot bring the bottles, a list with the names and how often you take them is the next best thing. Include the ones you were prescribed and quietly stopped. That fact changes the visit more than a complete family tree does.

Write the pharmacy’s name and phone on the same paper. Refills fail in the most ordinary way: the clinic sends them to a pharmacy you used three years ago.

## Papers that are worth the trouble

Bring a record only if it would change today. A recent lab. A hospital discharge from the last year. A blood pressure log. A list of allergies that includes the reaction, not just the word. You do not need every note ever written about you. If the important paper is missing, say which office has it. Do not send records through the website form. Call and ask how the clinic wants them.

Two questions are enough. People who bring twelve usually get through two anyway, and the other ten were the ones they were least worried about. Put the real ones at the top.

Insurance card and a photo ID if you have them. If you do not, you can still be seen at Canby Community Clinic. The address is 7601 Canby Ave #6B in Reseda, weekdays 9 to 5. Say you are new when you call, or say it at the desk. Leave a number you actually answer if you want a time held. The clinic calls back to set it.

The first minute can be plain. “I have not been seen in three years. My pressure at the pharmacy was high. I take this one pill.” That is a complete opening.

## The part people rehearse and do not need

You do not need a speech about every illness in the family. You need the ones a clinician would use today: a parent with early heart disease, diabetes, or a cancer that changes screening. If you do not know, “I don’t know” is an acceptable answer. Guessing a diagnosis you overheard at a funeral is how charts fill up with things that never happened.

You also do not need to fast unless someone told you to. You do not need to stop a daily medicine the morning of the visit unless you were told to hold it. If you are unsure, take it and say you took it.

Park with a few extra minutes. Suite 6B is not the front door of the building. If you use a wheelchair or you cannot do stairs, say so when you call and someone can tell you which entrance works.

When you leave, you should be able to say the next step in one sentence. A lab, a follow-up, a medicine continued, or nothing further. If you cannot say it, ask before you reach the sidewalk.
