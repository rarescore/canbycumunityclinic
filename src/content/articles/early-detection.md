# The result is only useful if someone is waiting for it

Screening looks for a problem before it is obvious. That is the hope. The part people skip is what happens after the paper comes back.

A stool test that is positive is not a cancer diagnosis. It is a reason for a colonoscopy. The [U.S. Preventive Services Task Force](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/colorectal-cancer-screening) says the benefit of that screening depends on follow-up when the finding is abnormal. The same shape shows up with mammograms, Pap tests, and a cholesterol number that was higher than expected. The first result points. It does not finish the story.

## Ask this before you leave

Where is the test done. Whether you need to prepare. Who calls you, and by when. What you should do if you hear nothing. If the next step is another office, the name of that office.

Write the answers on the same paper as the appointment. “No news is good news” drops results. If a week past the date you were given goes by, call.

Fear keeps people from the second appointment more often than confusion does. Say that out loud if it is true. A clinician can tell you what the result does and does not mean, and how soon the next step matters. Waiting because you would rather not know is understandable. It is also how a useful test turns into a missed one.
