# The card says Medi-Cal, and then it says something else

People call and say “I have Medi-Cal.” Often that is true and still incomplete. Medi-Cal coverage is frequently delivered through a health plan with its own name on the card. The person scheduling a visit may need that second name, not only the word Medi-Cal.

Have the card in front of you when you call. Read the plan name. If you are not sure which line is the plan and which line is a member number, read the plan and stop. Member numbers, dates of birth, and photos of the card do not belong in a website form or in ordinary email. Say them on the phone if someone at the clinic asks.

## If the card is new, or you cannot find it

New coverage takes time to show up in the systems a front desk checks. If you were approved and you do not have the plastic yet, say when you were approved and which plan you chose, if you chose one. [California’s Medi-Cal program](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/medi-cal) describes the program. Your plan’s own member line is who answers questions about which offices are in the network. Those are different phone calls.

Bring the card to the visit if you have it by then. Also bring the medicines you take. A coverage question and a medical question can happen on the same day. They are still different questions. The front desk can tell you what the card is. The clinician is who talks about the reason you came.

## If you were told two different things

It happens. A plan line says one thing, and the clinic says another, usually because the card is new, the plan name was misread, or an outside lab is a separate arrangement. Write down who said what, and the date. Call the clinic back with the plan name spelled out. Guessing from a screenshot is how people drive across the Valley for a visit that was never set.

Canby Community Clinic is at 7601 Canby Ave #6B, Reseda, weekdays 9 to 5, (818) 674-4414. If you want a time held, leave your name and phone. Someone calls you back.

## The rest of the path

A visit at the clinic and a test at an outside lab are often two arrangements. Ask, before you leave, where the blood draw happens and whether your plan is part of that step. People lose weeks because the visit went fine and the lab slip was for a place that would not take the card.

If you are helping a parent, bring them into the call or put the phone on speaker with their permission. A plan name you half-heard is how the wrong office gets the referral. Spell it. Ask the person on the phone to spell it back.

English and Spanish are both fine for that call. If you need another language, say so at the start. The point of the card is to get you into the right room. It is not a test you have to pass before anyone will talk to you.
